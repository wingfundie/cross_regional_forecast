# Improvement roadmap: data, concepts and experiments

Prepared 11 September 2026. **Everything below is proposed work, not a completed experiment or a claim of measured improvement.** Priorities are engineering judgement informed by the current results and the cited sources. The [methods guide](METHODS_AND_RESEARCH.md) describes what ran; the [backtest protocol](BACKTEST_PROTOCOL.md) defines the existing evidence.

## 1. Recommended order

1. Improve restriction detection and uncertainty using the existing data: dedicated event models, richer observed regime features, and calibration with a lead mixture matching evaluation.
2. Add issue-time outage/constraint information. This is the highest-priority extra-data hypothesis for directional limits, especially several days ahead.
3. Establish an operational-input track using archived forecasts and prospective collection. Keep the realised-input track as a separate diagnostic experiment.
4. Improve flow prediction with spatial generation, regional supply-stack and bid information available at issue time.
5. Explore more complex network/sequence models only after these additions beat simpler candidates on untouched data.

The present 72.3% coverage for nominal 80% intervals motivates better calibration. The 92/120 cells beating persistence does not mean every link, direction or horizon benefits. Prioritise the worst missed-restriction and flow-error cells rather than optimising only a NEM-wide average. No numerical improvement estimate is justified before running these experiments.

## 2. Extra data to investigate

The [constraint-derived network feature design](CONSTRAINT_NETWORK_FEATURES.md) develops the generator/constraint idea end to end: signed IC-equivalent sensitivities, a compact feature dictionary, near-binding envelopes, setter switching, data/version contracts and a VNI-first experiment plan. It is proposed work, not an implemented enhancement.

The primary scope remains public NEMWEB and Open-Meteo. Listing a candidate source below does **not** establish that a complete three-year, issue-time archive exists. Before building a feature, verify public access, table/version definitions, earliest coverage, cadence, release lag, revisions and join keys. Save issue time, effective time and retrieval time separately.

Open-Meteo documents [historical forecasts](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-forecast-api), [previous runs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/previous-runs-api) and [single runs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/single-runs-api).

Optional expansion beyond the original source scope: detailed TNSP topology, dynamic ratings and outage engineering data could support a physical network model, but access, licensing and historical availability require a separate feasibility decision. They are not prerequisites assumed by this roadmap and have not been acquired.

## 3. Concepts and methods to try

| Priority / proposal | Rationale and implementation concept | How to judge it / main risk |
|---|---|---|
| P1: dedicated restriction classifier plus severity model | Train event probability directly, with validation-selected weighting for missed events; separately predict the MW shortfall below threshold. Retain continuous limit forecasts. | Compare recall at the same false-alert budget, precision-recall curves, Brier and severity error. Class weighting can distort probabilities, so calibrate on a later untouched validation segment. |
| P1: direct quantile trees and conditional calibration | Fit P10/P50/P90 directly; compare with residual calibration conditioned on finer lead/regime groups and with rolling calibration using only matured outcomes. | Coverage by link/direction/lead plus width and pinball loss. Prevent quantile crossing and report any adjustment. Wider intervals alone are not an improvement. Sparse regimes need pooled fallback rather than unstable local estimates. |
| P1: persistence-change model and richer lag summaries | Predict change from the latest available limit/flow; add trailing minima, variability, ramps, time since setter change and duration below threshold. | Compare short-horizon errors and restriction onset/recovery. All trailing windows must stop at the publication-aware cutoff. |
| P1: align validation and training lead weights | Use all 336 validation leads where practical, or a stratified sample with explicit evaluation weights; compare lead-specific or narrower-band models. | Test whether broad-band calibration improves without excessive cost. Equal weighting per band and equal weighting per lead are different objectives; choose before evaluation. |
| P2: normal/restricted regime mixture | Predict regime probability, then combine specialised limit/flow models. Include negative/forced-flow states explicitly where samples support them. | Compare with one pooled tree and the direct classifier. Regimes must be inferred from known inputs, not supplied from future outcomes. Rare regimes may overfit. |
| P2: AEMO residual correction or horizon-dependent blending | Learn errors around issue-time AEMO forecasts; blend persistence, AEMO and trees with weights fitted on validation. | Compare on common origin/delivery pairs. AEMO input coverage may limit horizons; use an explicit fallback beyond coverage. |
| P2: separate flow and limit feature strategies | Limits may respond strongly to topology/constraint regimes; flow may respond more to bids, supply mix and regional residual-demand differences. Test connector-specific models against pooled models. | Report target-specific ablations. Do not assume one feature set or one model is best for every link and direction. |
| P2: coherent input scenarios | Propagate correlated demand, wind, solar and weather forecast paths through the models; add conditional model uncertainty carefully. | Evaluate trajectory coverage and joint multi-day restriction risk. Avoid independently sampling incompatible regional weather paths or double-counting uncertainty already in residuals. |
| P2: recency weighting and scheduled refitting | Compare expanding training, rolling windows and modest recency weights as the generation/network mix changes. | Use nested chronological evaluation and fully matured labels. Track catastrophic-event forgetting and degradation on rare restrictions. |
| P3: joint five-minute or structured target model | Forecast five-minute paths and aggregate, or model mean and nonnegative mean-minus-tight gaps to respect the target definition. | Test tight-limit recall, spike timing and mean errors. Half-hour mean and tight targets have structural relationships, but independent directional limits are not a secure dispatch envelope. Do not blindly clip flow into predicted limits. |
| P3: graph, sequence or physical/statistical hybrid | Network-aware or temporal models could capture cross-link coupling; an optimisation layer would require valid topology, constraint and dispatch inputs. | Start only after stronger tree/baseline comparisons. More complexity does not establish security feasibility; a partial network reconstruction can be misleading. |

LightGBM explicitly supports quantile regression through its objective and alpha parameters; see the [official parameter documentation](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/Parameters.html). The remaining designs above are proposed experiments, not reproductions of that documentation or evidence of NEM performance. The [methods guide's research table](METHODS_AND_RESEARCH.md#2-research-reviewed-and-how-it-influenced-the-implementation) gives the reviewed academic precedents and limits of transfer to this project.

Conformal-style recalibration is another possible experiment, but ordinary exchangeability-based coverage arguments do not automatically hold for overlapping time-series forecasts and network regime changes. Do not label intervals “guaranteed” simply because a conformal adjustment is added.

## 4. Better evaluation for the user's decision

Retain the agreed 50% seasonal-reference event for comparability. Add MW severity and event episodes so a long restriction is not counted only as thousands of repeated forecast pairs. Define episode construction before testing: consecutive restricted half-hours, a prespecified gap-merging rule, minimum duration if used, and a clear warning lead window. Report episode recall, warning lead time, duration/recovery error, false-alert episodes per day and missed restriction MW-hours. Calculate MW-hours once per delivery/episode or clearly state the forecast-origin weighting; do not silently sum repeated predictions of the same event.

Evaluate demand/renewable extremes, planned versus unplanned restrictions, forced-flow conditions, new constraint setters and commissioning transitions. Keep average and tight-limit performance separate. Consider sensitivity to alternative thresholds only as a supplementary analysis; do not retrospectively redefine success around the threshold that looks best.

For regional supply balance, combine coherent flow scenarios and local generation/demand scenarios. Pairwise marginal P10/P90 limits cannot simply be added to obtain regional secure import capacity. Price effects require an additional dispatch/economic or separately validated price model; associations with realised spreads do not identify causal effects of relaxing a limit.

## 5. Experiment governance and acceptance

March–August 2026 has already been examined. It is now a reference benchmark, not a fresh test for choosing improvements. Use chronological development folds inside earlier data where sufficient history exists, then reserve a new untouched period with matured seven-day outcomes. An independent full-year test requires more data collection; it cannot be created by relabelling already inspected months.

For every experiment record: hypothesis; data source/vintage coverage; allowed information at origin; feature and code versions; split dates; model settings; training budget; primary metric; false-alert constraint; all-connector guardrails; and the decision rule. Compare one data family or method change at a time on identical eligible pairs, then test useful combinations. Report reduced coverage when an extra source shortens the sample. Never compare different date mixes as if they were paired improvements.

Suggested acceptance policy, to freeze before testing: require paired gains on the chosen primary metric, uncertainty estimates using dependence-aware resampling, no material deterioration in critical link/direction cells, and acceptable runtime/data freshness. Exact allowable false alerts and MW deterioration remain business choices; no unapproved numeric tolerance is invented here. Because missed restrictions matter more, compare recall at a fixed alert burden as well as F2. Publish negative results and compare against persistence and the current frozen model throughout.

Keep two scoreboards: **conditional research** with declared realised inputs, and **operational simulation** using only demonstrably available forecast vintages. Operational errors may be higher even when the system becomes more credible and useful. Completing that transition is itself an improvement in evaluation validity, not a promise of lower headline MAE.
