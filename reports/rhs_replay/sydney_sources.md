# Finding local demand for the RHS calculator

The electrical boundary determines which series to inject. AEMO’s February 2026 point dictionary identifies `N_SYDNEY_LOAD` as a three-city aggregate covering Sydney, Wollongong and Newcastle. Transgrid also publishes actual half-hourly values for transformers at Sydney West and flows on individual lines there. Those three measurements answer different questions. No source checked here supplies a dated list of meters equating western Sydney, as a geographic area, with the AEMO point. [February EMSMASTER ZIP](https://www.nemweb.com.au/Data_Archive/Wholesale_Electricity/MMSDM/2026/MMSDM_2026_02/MMSDM_Historical_Data_SQLLoader/DATA/PUBLIC_ARCHIVE%23EMSMASTER%23FILE01%23202602010000.zip) · [Anonymous download checks and hashes](sydney_sources.csv)

For an equation that actually requests `N_SYDNEY_LOAD`, a public DispatchIS monitor provides a much better answer than assembling substations. For monitor `DATASNAP_DFS_NSYD`, effective 11 July 2024, version 1, subtract 10,000 from its published RHS. At interval ending 04:05 NEM time on 4 February 2026 the monitor gives 3,705.96655 MW, against 3,705.96655273438 MW in the dispatch case. The same report encoded `TERRANORA_LOAD` as `DATASNAP_TE_967_1` less 10,000, yielding 10.3 MW. The file records generation at 04:00:11, eleven seconds after the interval began. These formulas recover same-run historical inputs at source precision. For an interval-start calculation, retain the previous eligible report, its age, and the distinction between report generation and observed public receipt. The monitors are separate published equations; feeding a target equation its own RHS would be circular. [Versioned monitor checks](inventory/datasnap.csv)

The biggest problem with the Sydney West BSP workbook appears before any comparison with AEMO. The raw median was −613.83 in February 2025 and −689.21 among the available June records. The July 2025 median was +15.41. February 2026 values have a median of +10.45. The downloaded `Avg` worksheet labels its numeric column `TOT_TX`; neither that label nor the FAQ explains the change in scale. Changing the sign cannot reconcile these magnitudes. The measurements remain useful evidence for a question to the publisher about the meter mapping, units and sign across the July transition. The underlying cause is unresolved. [Checked values](sydney_sources.csv) · [Transgrid line and BSP FAQ](https://tapr.transgrid.com.au/)

Transgrid describes its traces as time-weighted half-hour averages. For line data, positive flow usually denotes export from the named end and negative flow import. The FAQ allows exceptions. The spreadsheet timestamps have no UTC offset and their labels do not specify which edge of the half-hour they mark. Preserve the original timestamps until the source convention is established. A simple mean of six five-minute snapshots is also only an approximation to a continuously time-weighted average. [Transgrid portal](https://tapr.transgrid.com.au/)

For a **Western Sydney Priority Growth Area** estimate, a Transgrid supply diagram shows contributions from Sydney West and West Liverpool. Switching, overlapping metering levels and downstream generation can change the apparent balance. For a **Sydney West BSP transformer** estimate, Transgrid’s February 2026 project assessment identifies the specific 330/132 kV site. For the **AEMO three-city demand** point, neither source defines all of its constituent circuits. An electrical sum would require a dated list of boundary meters, signs, embedded-generation treatment and topology, followed by validation against the five-minute point actually used by the equation. [Transgrid supply diagram, figure 2-3](https://www.transgrid.com.au/media/wwpo0dlu/transgrid-pacr_macarthur.pdf) · [February 2026 site assessment](https://www.transgrid.com.au/media/ab4kis3v/maintaining-reliable-supply-to-western-sydney_mcc-assessment_february-2026-1.pdf)

## Terranora as the local-demand replay

`TERRANORA_LOAD` is an analog measurement described by AEMO as the Terranora area load. Essential Energy owns the sub-transmission substation. It receives power over two 110 kV circuits from Queensland and can receive or export power through Directlink. The physical interconnector boundary flow and actual Directlink transfer can therefore differ by local demand and losses. Substituting a nearby Transgrid site or the border flow alone would omit part of this balance. [Essential Energy network description](https://www.essentialenergy.com.au/-/media/Project/EssentialEnergy/Website/Files/Our-Network/DAPR-Essential-Energy-2025.pdf?rev=929391b9ac8a4885b9fdca4e5a998b81) · [AEMO capability definition](https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/electricity/nem/security_and_reliability/congestion-information/2024/interconnector-capabilities.pdf)

Two named AEMO points could support a historical electrical balance. `Q_DIRLK_MW` measures Directlink transfer from Bungalora to Terranora. `Q_DIRLK_T174TA757_758` measures both border feeders at the Terranora end. The second description leaves the positive direction unspecified. `Q_T174_TRA_LOAD` describes the sum of three transformer loads and appears under `Z_ARCHIVE`. AEMO’s metadata supplies no equality between that point and `TERRANORA_LOAD`. Any substitute would need the orientation, the applicable losses and ancillary loads, simultaneous input values and a comparison with the direct historical point. [February point definitions](sydney_sources.csv)

Essential Energy offers half-hourly kW and kVAr for individual zone substations, stamped in AEST and released annually. Its public index listed coverage through 30 September 2025 when checked. There was no February 2026 download on that index or documented aggregation of these downstream sites into `TERRANORA_LOAD`. Powerlink’s planning statement contains a load-duration curve, which discards interval order. Neither product qualified as a matched half-hour benchmark for these replay windows. [Essential Energy data index](https://www.essentialenergy.com.au/our-network/network-projects/zone-substation-reports) · [Powerlink planning statement](https://www.aer.gov.au/documents/powerlink-2027-32-cp03196-bs1009-mudgeeraba-terranora-refit-january-2026)

## Regional demand as a proxy

AEMO’s operational demand forecasts by area use a single NSW forecast area. In the dispatch results, `DISPATCHREGIONSUM.INITIALSUPPLY` measures scheduled and semi-scheduled supply plus net imports at the interval start. A share calibrated against a local point can be tested economically without downloading a complete feeder network. Its possible errors include differences in local weather, rooftop solar and industrial load, and changes to switching. The timestamp attached to a dispatch result alone cannot establish that it was available when dispatch started. Use a proven earlier publication or mark the calculation retrospective. The study’s replay tables evaluate the selected proxies against the actual input. [AEMO load-forecasting procedure](https://aemo.com.au/-/media/files/electricity/nem/security_and_reliability/power_system_ops/procedures/so_op_3710-load-forecasting.pdf) · [AEMO demand definitions](https://aemo.com.au/-/media/Files/Electricity/NEM/Security_and_Reliability/Dispatch/Policy_and_Process/Demand-terms-in-EMMS-Data-Model.pdf)

The download checks can be repeated from the project root with `python -m scripts.rhs_sydney_sources`. The script reads the two Transgrid workbooks, the point dictionary and the Endeavour catalogue in memory, then writes the compact source evidence CSV. Retrieval times record when this study accessed each source. They do not establish original publication or receipt in February.
